Networks and costs

#Devnet, testnet, mainnet

Solana runs several separate networks. They use the same software but have different data and different SOL.

NetworkWhat it is forSOLData
devnettrying things outfree from a faucet, worth nothingcan be reset; old data may disappear
testnettesting the network software itselffree, worth nothingreset from time to time
mainnet-betathe real networkreal moneypermanent

chainboot starts on devnet. Switch with:

chainboot network                  # show the current network
chainboot network mainnet-beta     # switch (also: devnet, testnet)

Each network has its own list of RPC endpoints, its own login and its own vault. chainboot checks every endpoint you add and refuses one that belongs to a different network, because mixing networks would silently break uploads.

#Other chains

chainboot is not tied to Solana's own three networks. It works on any chain that runs Solana programs and answers Solana's RPC (these are often called SVM chains; Eclipse, Sonic and SOON are examples). Adding one takes a name and one endpoint:

chainboot network add eclipse https://mainnetbeta-rpc.eclipse.xyz
chainboot network list
chainboot network devnet            # back to Solana's devnet

chainboot asks the endpoint which chain it serves and remembers the chain by its genesis hash, so every endpoint you add later is checked against it. Then everything works as on Solana: publish, list, run, save, share. Each chain has its own logins, vault and saves.

What is different there:

  • Transactions are smaller. chainboot uses the transaction format every such chain has, 1232 bytes instead of Solana's newer 4096, so the same data takes about four times as many transactions.
  • The carrier may be the Memo program. On Solana the data rides in instructions for the Noop program, which takes any bytes. Where Noop is not deployed (chainboot checks when you add the chain), the Memo program carries it as base64 text, which needs a third more room and far more compute. Readers understand both without a setting.
  • No official catalog. Name a publisher (<address>/<image>) or publish your own.
  • Fees are the chain's own, in its own currency.

Honest status: adding chains and reading from them was tried against the public endpoints of Eclipse, Sonic and SOON. Writing through the Memo program was tried on Solana devnet, which has the same program; nothing has been published on another chain yet.

A chain that is not SVM (Ethereum and its relatives, for example) would need a second implementation of four things: putting bytes into a signed transaction, fetching a transaction by its ID, checking who signed it, and listing the transactions that name a tag. The formats above that layer (The on-chain format) would stay as they are.

#What is where today

  • Devnet: the official catalog with Solinux and Alpine. This is where chainboot was built and measured.
  • Mainnet: no official catalog yet, and chainboot has not been exercised there. The 4096-byte transaction format chainboot writes has been active on mainnet since September 2026 according to Solana's upgrade notes. Everything works the same way with your own images: publish with your own key, and run with chainboot run <your-address>/<image>.

#What storing data costs

Solana charges a fixed fee per transaction: 5,000 lamports = 0.000005 SOL. It does not matter how much data the transaction carries. chainboot fills each transaction with 3,912 bytes, so:

Amount of (compressed) dataTransactionsFee
1 kB (a note, a pointer)10.000005 SOL
100 kB270.00014 SOL
1 MB2610.0013 SOL
100 MB26,1000.13 SOL
1 GB267,0001.34 SOL

Real examples from devnet (rounded):

Reading is free of fees. You only need an RPC endpoint.

chainboot always shows the number of transactions and the fee before it sends anything (chainboot publish --dry-run, chainboot save).

#On a busy mainnet

When many people use the network, transactions with a small extra tip (a "priority fee") are included first. Turn it on with one setting:

chainboot priority-fee 2000      # lamports added to every transaction
chainboot priority-fee           # show the setting
chainboot priority-fee off

The tip applies to publishing and saving and is included in every cost estimate. A tip of 1,000 to 10,000 lamports is usually plenty; with 2,000 lamports, storing 1 MB costs 0.0018 SOL instead of 0.0013. Without a tip, uploads still complete, because chainboot sends again whatever did not land, but they can take longer. In the SDK: new ChainStore(pool, { priorityFeeLamports }), or per call.

#Accounts: a deposit instead of a fee

Everything above is about transactions: you pay once and the data is in the ledger. Solana has a second kind of storage, accounts. An account holds only a current value, every endpoint keeps it in memory, and one request reads it. It is not paid for but backed by a deposit ("rent") of about 7 SOL per megabyte, which comes back when the account is closed.

That makes accounts wrong for systems (Solinux would need 800 SOL) and right for the few small records that are read all the time: the pointers that say "this is my newest catalog" or "this is the newest save of this machine". chainboot can keep them in accounts as well:

chainboot accounts on            # from now on, pointers you write are also kept in accounts
chainboot accounts               # show the setting
chainboot accounts close         # close them and get the deposits back

Each pointer account holds a deposit of about 0.0033 SOL. Readers need no setting: they look for the account first, check the owner's signature inside it, and read the transactions as before if there is none. The pointer is still written to the ledger too, in the same transaction, so nothing depends on the account staying open. chainboot deploys no program for this; it uses the SPL Record program, which is on devnet and mainnet.

#How fast is it

Downloading depends mostly on your RPC endpoints. Measured with Solinux minimal (21.6 MB):

EndpointsTime to download and verify
13 endpoints of several providersabout 1 second
one endpoint of a provider with an API key (two were tried)0.7 to 1.5 seconds
only the public api.devnet.solana.comabout 100 seconds

The public endpoints are meant for light use and limit requests strictly. A free account at any provider is the single biggest speed-up.

A lazy machine does not wait for the download at all: the full Solinux (114 MB on chain, 504 MB of files) answers its first command 6.7 seconds after chainboot run solinux:lazy --lazy on a computer that has nothing yet, against 18.4 seconds the ordinary way.

Finding the newest pointer (the first step of every list, run and restore) takes one request when its owner keeps it in an account, instead of a history lookup and a second request: on devnet about 0.3 seconds instead of 0.6.

Uploading runs at several hundred transactions per second with a handful of endpoints: Solinux minimal takes about 20 seconds, plus 40 seconds to compress it.

#How long does data stay

This is the honest part.

  • The ledger is permanent in the sense that the network agreed on it and it cannot be changed. chainboot data is part of the ledger.
  • But a validator does not need old transactions to do its work, and most keep only recent ones. Reading old transactions depends on RPC providers that keep the full history ("archive" nodes). On mainnet the large providers do. On devnet history is kept less carefully and the whole network can be reset.
  • chainboot can only read what your endpoints can serve. If an endpoint has no history, chainboot notices ("no data") and uses your other endpoints.

For anything you care about: use mainnet, use providers with archive access, and keep in mind that you rely on at least one of them continuing to serve history. Your own archive is ~/.chainboot/cache: everything you downloaded is there, verified, and you could publish it again.

#Moving from devnet to mainnet

  1. chainboot network mainnet-beta
  2. chainboot rpc add <a mainnet endpoint>
  3. Publish your images again with a funded mainnet keypair. Images do not move between networks by themselves, but the same archive gives the same digest, so anyone can check that the mainnet image is the one from devnet.
  4. Sign in again (chainboot login). Your mainnet vault is a different account than your devnet vault; fund it with real SOL.
